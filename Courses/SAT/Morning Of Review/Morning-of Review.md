## 1. The four execution rules

**Never leave a blank.** No wrong-answer penalty. A blank is a guaranteed zero; a guess is a
free 25%. Guess-and-flag, never blank-and-flag.

**A "leaning" feeling is not evidence.** On Practice 11 you went **4 for 14** on questions where
you'd narrowed to two and picked the one that felt right — worse than a coin flip. When you
feel that, go back to the text for one concrete line that decides it. If you can't find one,
flag it and move on.

**One question at a time.** Not "I have 27 questions." Just Question 12. Then Question 13.

**Three passes.** Pass 1: everything straightforward. Pass 2: flagged items, most-confident
first. Pass 3: whatever's left.

## 3. Grammar: the two rules that actually cost you

**All four choices are forms of the same verb?** That's not a tense question. Find the main
verb. If the sentence already has one and there's no FANBOYS or subordinator, the blank is a
**participle** (*prioritizing*, *searching*), not a finite verb.

**An interrupter closes with the mark that opened it.** Dash…dash, comma…comma. Never
dash…comma or dash…colon. The opening mark is usually outside the underline — **look left.**

Two more that solve questions in seconds:
- **Period and semicolon are identical.** If both appear as choices, cross out both.
- **Look left of a colon**, never right. Everything before it must be a complete sentence.

## 4. Math: what's on the reference sheet vs. what isn't

**Given:** circle area/circumference, rectangle, triangle, Pythagorean, 30-60-90 (`x, x√3, 2x`),
45-45-90 (`s, s, s√2`), volumes (box, cylinder, sphere, cone, pyramid), 360°, 2π radians, 180°.

**Not given — the ones that bit you:**
- Vertex form `y = a(x − h)² + k` → vertex `(h, k)` — **sign flips**
- Circle `(x − h)² + (y − k)² = r²` → center `(h, k)`, radius `r` — **sign flips, and it's r not r²**
- `sin θ = opp/hyp` — label the sides **relative to the named angle** before choosing the ratio
- Rational function: `g(k) = 0` → the **numerator** is zero at k
- Sum = mean × count

## 5. Desmos: when to reach for it

Verified from your own misses and near-misses — type it in rather than solving by hand when
you see:

- **A system of equations** — graph both, read the intersection. (Would have settled M2 Q19.)
- **Reading a value off a provided graph** — retype the line and click the gray intercept dots
  instead of eyeballing the gridlines. (M1 Q19 was an intercept misread.)
- **Any "for what value of x" with ugly numbers**
- **Vertex, max/min, or roots** — click the point, don't derive it
- **A quadratic whose answers aren't integers**

Desmos is available for **both** Math modules, the whole time.
