# Bluebook Parity, Khan Academy, Strategy, and Social Advice Research (2026-08-12)

Four-angle research pass for the "Bluebook-identical test + diagnostics + crash courses" build, ahead of the 2026-08-22 SAT date (10 days out as of this writing). Does not repeat what's already solid in the 2026-08-07/08 research files (structure/timing/domain weightings) — fills in Bluebook tooling detail, corrects a stale assumption about Khan Academy, firms up pacing/strategy consensus, and covers current social-media advice.

## 1. Official CollegeBoard / Bluebook Guidance

**Already confirmed in existing research** (not re-derived here): 2 modules/section, R&W 64 min/54 Q, Math 70 min/44 Q, adaptive M1→M2 routing, domain question-count ranges, Student Question Bank, Bluebook test count.

**New findings:**

- **Scoring:** total 400–1600, section 200–800 each. Raw score (# correct, **no wrong-answer penalty**) → scaled via IRT equating that accounts for which M2 difficulty tier the student was routed into.
- **Calculator is the real Desmos engine**, licensed and embedded in Bluebook, available for the entire Math section (both modules) — not a College-Board clone.
- **Annotation/highlighting tool** highlights/underlines passage or question text. Separate **Line Reader tool**: a draggable "digital ruler" that isolates one line at a time.
- **Mark for Review:** bookmark flag per question, color-coded status bar always visible, full review screen at end of each module (flagged/unanswered/answered), jump-to-flagged before submitting.
- **Option Eliminator ("cross-out"):** toggled on via an icon; crosses out (visually struck or hidden) a choice per question; fully undoable. A real interactive tool, not just a mental habit — worth demoing, not just describing.
- **Math reference sheet is geometry-only** (~15 formulas: area/volume, Pythagorean theorem, special right triangles, circle constants). Algebra, functions, statistics, and most geometry are **not** on it — must be memorized. Concrete, testable fact for the crash course.

**"Bluebook-identical" checklist** (what a faithful mock actually needs):
1. Timing down to the module (32 min R&W, 35 min Math) with an untimed break between sections.
2. Working Mark-for-Review + review screen (flag → jump-to-flagged at module end).
3. Working Option Eliminator (cross-out with undo).
4. Real Desmos widget, module-wide, for Math.
5. Geometry-only reference sheet, exactly as scoped (common third-party-mock error: over-providing formulas).
6. Annotation/highlight + line-reader for R&W.
7. Two unscored, unidentifiable pretest questions per module (cosmetic only).

Items 4 and 5 are the ones homemade mocks most often get wrong.

## 2. Khan Academy's Official SAT Practice

- **Correction to a stale assumption:** Khan Academy **no longer hosts full-length practice tests**. Those now live exclusively in Bluebook. Bluebook links back into KA for missed-question rationales and routed practice after a test.
- **What KA still does:** thousands of official practice questions, skill-targeted drills, instructional videos, diagnostic-driven "Personalized Practice."
- **Diagnostic structure:** 8 short quizzes (4 Math, 2 Reading, 2 Writing), ~5–10 questions each. Offers an **"I would be guessing" option** instead of forcing a guess, to avoid corrupting the skill model with lucky/unlucky guesses.
- **Recommendation engine:** after diagnostics (or an uploaded score, or a completed practice test), KA serves item-level, difficulty-calibrated recommendations per skill — not just "more Advanced Math," but calibrated within it.
- **Worth mimicking pedagogically:** the "guessing flag" (Diagnostic Gap Lessons' ✅/⚠️/❌/⬜ legend already does this narratively) and difficulty-calibrated re-serving (stretch goal, hard to hand-build in 10 days — more relevant long-term).

## 3. Test-Taking Strategy & Third-Party Resources

**Pacing consensus** (now corroborated across multiple independent sources, still community not CollegeBoard-published): R&W ≈60–90 sec/question (reading passages ~85–90s, standalone grammar ~45–60s); Math ≈90–100 sec/question.

**Guessing/flagging strategy:** finish every question in the module first (guess-and-flag, never leave-blank-and-flag — a blank is guaranteed zero, a guess is nonzero-chance with zero penalty). Then work back through flagged items **most-confident to least-confident** — bank easy points first.

**Third-party resources, ranked for digital-format specificity:**
- *The Official Digital SAT Study Guide* (College Board) — genuinely official content, 4 official practice tests.
- Princeton Review Premium Prep — 848pp, 6 full-lengths (3 print + 3 adaptive-online).
- Barron's SAT 2026 (Brian W. Stewart) — rebuilt for the digital adaptive format, 1,800+ questions.
- Erica Meltzer's *Ultimate Guide to SAT Grammar* — best-in-class for Conventions/grammar, directly relevant to Donovan's confirmed weak spot.
- UWorld — highest-volume drilling resource, well-suited to a final-weeks push.
- OnePrep (already flagged in 2026-08-07 research) — best free, UI-matched supplementary volume source.

**Reddit r/SAT consensus:** rehearse under fully realistic conditions (timed, no breaks beyond official, no phone/music) — validates a genuinely Bluebook-identical mock as the capstone, not a nice-to-have.

**Real complaint, not just upselling:** Bluebook's own free practice pool reportedly runs easier than the real test in algebra/data-analysis/word-problems — supplementing with KA's item bank, UWorld, or curated sets is standard advice.

## 4. Social Media Advice (Recent)

*(Gathered via WebSearch — the `last30days` skill requires an interactive setup wizard with live consent prompts not appropriate to run unattended for a research task. A follow-up session where Donovan answers its prompts directly would get genuinely fresh 30-day social-listening data with sourced quotes.)*

- **Popular digital-SAT TikTok creators:** Ryan Choice (@ryan.choice, ~2.1M) — grammar-hack "getting to 1600" content. Solutions with Sahil (@solutionswithsahil, ~984K) — general hacks framing. Shorter-form, higher-virality than the YouTube channels already vetted in the 2026-08-07 research (satgamified, PenguinTestPrep, JamesLu) — likely lower depth per video.
- **Snake-oil flag:** a circulating TikTok trend brands itself "Simple Super Awesome Trick (S.A.T.)" claiming flat 40–60 point boosts from one technique. Treat any single-technique "guaranteed N-point jump" claim as a red flag. Legitimate 10-day gains come from closing specific diagnosed gaps (the existing Diagnostic Gap Lessons approach), not a viral trick.
- **Genuinely corroborated (not hype):** "practice under real timed conditions" and "use Mark-for-Review + Option Eliminator deliberately" — consistent across TikTok, Reddit, and prep blogs, and independently converge with Section 3 above.
- **Common complaint (r/SAT, current cycle):** the harder-routed second Math module feels disproportionately difficult, and R&W passages feel like "word games." Consistent with (not contradictory to) the adaptive-routing mechanic already documented — worth telling Donovan directly so a hard-feeling M2 doesn't rattle him: it usually means he's in the upper band, not that something's wrong.

## Must-Get-Right vs. Nice-to-Have (Bluebook-identical mock)

**Must get right:**
1. Exact timing per module (32/35 min), real countdown.
2. Working Mark-for-Review + end-of-module review screen with jump-to-flagged.
3. Working Option Eliminator (cross-out, undoable) — changes reading behavior, not cosmetic.
4. Real Desmos widget for all of Math.
5. Geometry-only reference sheet, scoped exactly as CollegeBoard scopes it.
6. No-penalty scoring displayed correctly (raw = # correct, not # correct − # wrong).
7. Domain question-count weightings matching the existing Table 10/16 research.

**Nice-to-have:**
1. Annotation/highlight tool and line-reader.
2. Two unidentifiable unscored pretest questions per module.
3. Adaptive M1→M2 routing itself — hard to replicate without real item-response calibration; a fixed-difficulty mock is an acceptable 10-day simplification, as long as Donovan knows the real test would route him.
4. Exact Bluebook visual chrome/UI — mechanics (timing, tools, scoring) matter far more than pixels.
