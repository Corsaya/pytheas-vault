# R&W — Information and Ideas Crash Course

Information and Ideas is ~26% of Reading & Writing (12–14 questions) per
`Official SAT Structure and Content Research` — roughly tied with Craft and
Structure for the single biggest R&W domain. Covers four skills from the
Foundations Knowledge Check: **I1** central idea/purpose, **I2** command of
evidence (textual), **I3** command of evidence (quantitative), and **I4**
inference. All four share one passage format — a single short passage
(usually 25–150 words) followed by one question — so the same reading habits
carry across all of them.

The shared meta-strategy from the vetted YouTube research
(`Research/YouTube Guide Research`): **Question → Passage → Prediction →
Elimination.** Read the question stem first so you know what you're looking
for before you read the passage, form your own answer in your head before
looking at the choices, then eliminate rather than hunt for "the right one."
For I3 specifically: don't pre-study the graph/table — read the passage and
question first, then consult the data only to check the specific choices.

### I1 — Central idea / main purpose of a passage

**What this actually tests:** can you identify the single thing the passage
as a whole is doing — not a detail from the middle of it, but the point of
the whole passage. Digital SAT phrasing is consistent: "Which choice best
states the main idea/purpose of the text?" or "the main purpose of the text
is to ___."

**Strategy:**
1. Read the passage once, asking after each sentence "what is this sentence
   *doing* for the passage, not just what does it say."
2. After the last sentence, say the main idea in your own words in one
   short sentence — a purpose verb (argue, explain, describe, challenge,
   compare) plus the topic.
3. Match your sentence to a choice. Eliminate any choice that's too
   narrow (true of only one sentence, not the whole passage), too broad
   (goes beyond what the passage actually covers), or that reverses the
   passage's actual stance.

**Worked example**

> For decades, marine biologists assumed that octopuses, like most
> invertebrates, operated largely on instinct. Recent laboratory studies,
> however, have documented octopuses using tools, solving multi-step
> puzzles to access food, and even displaying distinct problem-solving
> "styles" from one individual to the next. These findings have pushed
> researchers to reconsider long-held assumptions about which parts of the
> animal kingdom are capable of flexible, individualized thinking.

**Question:** Which choice best states the main purpose of the text?

A) To argue that octopuses are more intelligent than other marine animals
B) To describe a specific puzzle-solving experiment conducted on octopuses
C) To explain how new research on octopus behavior has challenged prior
   assumptions about invertebrate cognition
D) To criticize earlier marine biologists for underestimating octopuses

**Reasoning:** The passage moves from an old assumption (instinct-only) to
new evidence (tool use, puzzles, individual styles) to a conclusion
(researchers reconsidering assumptions) — that arc *is* the main idea, and
C names all three pieces without adding anything the passage didn't say.

- A is too strong/off-topic — the passage never compares octopuses to other
  marine animals specifically, and never claims they're "more intelligent."
- B is too narrow — the puzzle-solving is one supporting detail, not the
  whole passage's purpose.
- D imports a judgmental tone ("criticize") that isn't in the passage — it
  describes a shift in scientific understanding, not a rebuke of past
  scientists.

**Trips people up:** the correct answer is usually the most boring,
complete summary — resist choices that sound more exciting or dramatic than
the passage's actual, measured tone.

### I2 — Command of evidence (textual)

**What this actually tests:** given a claim (either stated in the passage
or given to you in the question), can you find the exact quotation from the
passage that most directly supports it. Phrasing: "Which quotation from
[a work] would provide the best evidence for/illustration of the claim?" or
sometimes the passage states a claim and the question asks which of four
following sentences best supports it.

**Strategy:**
1. Identify exactly what the claim says — underline the key term(s) in the
   claim itself. The right quote has to support *that specific* claim, not
   the general topic.
2. Predict what kind of evidence would prove it before reading choices
   (a number, a named example, a described outcome, etc.).
3. Eliminate quotes that are on-topic but don't actually prove the specific
   claim — this is the single most common trap on this skill.

**Worked example**

> A growing number of city planners argue that adding more traffic lanes
> to relieve congestion is often self-defeating. When a highway is widened,
> more drivers who previously avoided it — or used other routes, or
> carpooled, or took transit — shift back onto the road, and within a few
> years congestion returns to its earlier level. This phenomenon, sometimes
> called "induced demand," has been documented in cities from Los Angeles
> to Houston to Seoul.

**Claim:** Some researchers contend that widening highways does not
produce a lasting reduction in traffic congestion.

**Question:** Which quotation from the text most effectively illustrates
this claim?

A) "adding more traffic lanes to relieve congestion is often
   self-defeating"
B) "more drivers who previously avoided it...shift back onto the road, and
   within a few years congestion returns to its earlier level"
C) "This phenomenon...has been documented in cities from Los Angeles to
   Houston to Seoul"
D) "A growing number of city planners argue" this about traffic lanes

**Reasoning:** The claim is specifically about the *outcome not lasting* —
congestion coming back. B is the only quote that states that mechanism and
outcome directly: drivers return, congestion returns "within a few years."

- A is close but only restates the general claim in vaguer terms ("often
  self-defeating") — it doesn't show *why* or *how*, so it's evidence-light,
  more of a paraphrase than a proof.
- C is true and about the same topic but only tells you the phenomenon has
  been "documented" somewhere — it doesn't itself describe what happens,
  so it doesn't directly support the claim's content.
- D just tells you planners hold this view — an appeal to who said it, not
  evidence of the mechanism itself.

**Trips people up:** the right answer must directly support the *specific*
claim as worded, not just be generally relevant to the topic — a classic
wrong choice is a true, on-topic quote that doesn't actually prove the
particular thing being claimed (like A and C above).

### I3 — Command of evidence (quantitative)

**What this actually tests:** given a claim and a graph/table, can you read
the data correctly to determine whether it supports, contradicts, or is
irrelevant to the claim. Per the vetted research: use "graph minimalism" —
don't pre-analyze the whole table before you know the question; read the
claim/question first, then go find only the specific numbers that answer it.

**Data:**

| Year | Households with a smart thermostat (%) | Average winter energy bill ($) |
|---|---|---|
| 2019 | 8% | 210 |
| 2020 | 14% | 205 |
| 2021 | 22% | 198 |
| 2022 | 31% | 189 |
| 2023 | 41% | 183 |

**Passage claim:** A researcher claims that as smart thermostat adoption
has increased, average winter energy bills have steadily decreased.

**Question:** Which choice best describes how the data in the table
relate to the researcher's claim?

A) The data support the claim: as the percentage of households with smart
   thermostats rose each year, average winter energy bills fell each year.
B) The data contradict the claim: smart thermostat adoption increased
   while energy bills also increased.
C) The data are insufficient to evaluate the claim because the table does
   not include data from before 2019.
D) The data partially support the claim, but bills increased in 2021.

**Reasoning:** Read down both columns year by year: adoption rises every
single year (8→14→22→31→41), and the bill column falls every single year
(210→205→198→189→183) with no exception. That's a clean, direct match to
"steadily decreased" — A is correct.

- B is a straightforward misread — bills go down, not up; check the actual
  direction of each column instead of assuming.
- C invents a requirement (pre-2019 data) the claim never asked for — the
  claim is about the trend across the years actually shown, and the table
  is sufficient to check that.
- D describes an exception (2021 increase) that isn't in the table — every
  year from 2019 to 2023 shows a bill decrease, so this misstates the data.

**Trips people up:** wrong choices often describe a plausible-sounding
trend that isn't actually in the table (a partial exception, a missing
range) — trace the actual numbers row by row instead of trusting a choice
that just "sounds like" a reasonable data story.

### I4 — Inference

**What this actually tests:** what can you reasonably conclude from a
passage that the passage doesn't state outright. Phrasing: "Which choice
most logically completes the text?" (often the last sentence is blanked
out) or "the text most strongly suggests that ___."

**Strategy:**
1. Read the passage and identify exactly what *is* stated as fact.
2. Ask: what is the smallest, most cautious next step the stated facts
   point to? Not the most interesting possibility — the safest one.
3. Only pick answers with direct textual support for every part of the
   claim — eliminate anything that requires an outside assumption the
   passage doesn't give you the pieces for.

**Worked example**

> Archaeologists excavating a Bronze Age settlement found cooking hearths
> inside nearly every dwelling they uncovered, along with communal ovens in
> the settlement's central plaza. Isotopic analysis of food residue in the
> household hearths showed a wide variety of grains and meats, while
> residue from the communal ovens was almost exclusively bread-related.

**Question:** Which choice most logically completes the text below?

Based on this evidence, the archaeologists most reasonably concluded that
___

A) most food preparation in the settlement was done by a small,
   specialized group who worked exclusively at the communal ovens
B) the communal ovens were used mainly for a narrower, bread-focused
   purpose distinct from the more varied cooking done in individual
   households
C) the settlement's residents preferred bread to any other food
D) individual households eventually stopped using their hearths in favor
   of the communal ovens

**Reasoning:** The passage gives two facts: household hearths show varied
grain/meat residue, communal ovens show almost exclusively bread residue.
The cautious inference is just that these two spaces served different,
specific purposes — B restates that gap without adding anything unstated.

- A invents a "specialized group" of bakers that the passage never
  mentions — nothing here tells us *who* used the ovens, only *what* was
  cooked in them.
- C is a leap from "bread was cooked there" to "residents preferred bread"
  — preference isn't supported by residue data alone; frequency of cooking
  isn't the same as preference.
- D invents a timeline ("eventually stopped") the passage gives zero
  evidence for — there's no before/after information here at all.

**Trips people up:** a valid SAT inference is always a small, cautious step
beyond what's stated, never a leap — over-inferring (adding a cause, a
motive, a timeline, or a "who" the passage never gives you) is the classic
wrong-answer trap, not under-inferring.

## Mini-Diagnostic

**MD-I1.** Which choice best states the main purpose of the text?

> Honeybee colonies communicate the location of food sources through a
> "waggle dance" — a series of movements performed on the honeycomb that
> encode both the direction and distance of a nearby flower patch relative
> to the sun. First described in detail in the mid-20th century, the dance
> remains one of the clearest known examples of symbolic communication in a
> non-human species, and continues to shape how researchers study
> communication systems across the animal kingdom.

A) To argue that honeybees are the most intelligent insect species
B) To describe the waggle dance as an example of non-human symbolic
   communication and its lasting influence on research
C) To explain the exact physical steps of the waggle dance in detail
D) To compare honeybee communication to human language systems

**MD-I2.** Which quotation from the text most effectively illustrates the
claim that urban tree cover provides a measurable cooling benefit?

> City planning departments have increasingly promoted tree-planting
> initiatives as a response to rising summer temperatures. Trees offer many
> benefits beyond aesthetics — they support local wildlife, reduce
> stormwater runoff, and, according to a multi-city study, neighborhoods
> with tree canopy coverage above 30% recorded average surface temperatures
> 4–6 degrees cooler than neighborhoods with less than 10% coverage during
> peak summer months. Advocacy groups have used such findings to push for
> expanded funding.

A) "City planning departments have increasingly promoted tree-planting
   initiatives"
B) "Trees offer many benefits beyond aesthetics"
C) "neighborhoods with tree canopy coverage above 30% recorded average
   surface temperatures 4–6 degrees cooler than neighborhoods with less
   than 10% coverage"
D) "Advocacy groups have used such findings to push for expanded funding"

**MD-I3.** A claim states: "A nutritionist argues that as daily added-sugar
intake has declined nationally, rates of a certain metabolic condition have
also declined." Does the table support, contradict, or fail to address the
claim?

| Year | Avg. daily added sugar (g) | Metabolic condition rate (%) |
|---|---|---|
| 2018 | 82 | 11.2% |
| 2019 | 79 | 11.4% |
| 2020 | 75 | 11.6% |
| 2021 | 70 | 11.9% |
| 2022 | 65 | 12.3% |

A) The data support the claim — both values decrease together over time.
B) The data contradict the claim — sugar intake fell while the condition
   rate rose each year.
C) The data are insufficient because the table doesn't include data before
   2018.
D) The data partially support the claim, with one exception in 2020.

**MD-I4.** Which choice most logically completes the text?

> A publisher tracked reader reviews for a new novel across its first three
> printings. In the first printing, reviewers frequently praised the
> book's pacing but criticized its ending as abrupt. In response, the
> publisher issued a revised ending for the second printing; reviews of the
> second printing no longer mentioned the ending as a complaint, though
> praise for the pacing continued unchanged.

Based on this pattern, it can most reasonably be concluded that ___

A) the revised ending resolved the specific complaint about abruptness
   without affecting readers' views of the book's pacing
B) the second printing sold significantly more copies than the first
C) reviewers of the second printing considered the book's pacing to be its
   weakest element
D) the publisher plans to revise the ending again for a third printing

---

### Answer key

- **MD-I1: B** — the passage introduces the waggle dance, explains what it
  encodes, and closes on its ongoing research influence; A and D aren't
  supported (no comparison to other species/humans made this way), and C
  describes only a supporting detail, not the whole passage's purpose.
- **MD-I2: C** — it's the only quote containing the actual comparison
  (canopy % vs. temperature difference) that proves the specific "cooling
  benefit" claim; A, B, and D are on-topic but don't contain the
  supporting data itself.
- **MD-I3: B** — sugar intake falls every year (82→65) while the condition
  rate rises every year (11.2%→12.3%) — the opposite of the claim, so it's
  a direct contradiction, not a partial match or missing data.
- **MD-I4: A** — the passage gives exactly two facts (ending complaint
  disappeared, pacing praise unchanged) and A restates only those two
  facts; B, C, and D each add information (sales, relative weakness,
  future plans) the passage never provides.
