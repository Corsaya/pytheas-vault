# Answer Key

## Math

**Q1 — C (14).** $3(2x-4)=6x-12=5x+2 \Rightarrow x=14$. Check: $3(2\cdot14-4)=3(24)=72$, $5(14)+2=72$ ✓
> **Trap:** (D) 18 comes from adding instead of subtracting when isolating $x$; (A) 2 comes from distributing only to the first term.

**Q2 — $-7$.** $y=3x+b$; $-1=3(2)+b=6+b \Rightarrow b=-7$.
> **Trap:** plugging the point into $y=mx+b$ backwards (solving for $x$-intercept instead) is the usual slip.

**Q3 — A.** $3x+2y=12 \Rightarrow 2y=-3x+12 \Rightarrow y=-\frac32x+6$.
> **Trap:** (C) forgets to divide the constant by 2; (D) flips the sign of $m$ but not the constant.

**Q4 — C (10).** No solution ⇒ coefficient ratios equal, constant ratio different: $\frac{2}{4}=\frac{5}{k} \Rightarrow k=10$. Confirm not identical: $\frac{10}{8}=\frac54 \ne \frac12$ ✓
> **Trap:** solving as if the system has a unique solution instead of recognizing the no-solution condition from ratios.

**Q5 — 5.** $30+45m=255 \Rightarrow 45m=225 \Rightarrow m=5$.
> **Trap:** forgetting to subtract the flat fee before dividing by the monthly rate.

**Q6 — B ($x \ge -4$).** $-2x \le 8 \Rightarrow x \ge -4$ (flip on divide-by-negative).
> **Trap:** (A) $x\le-4$ is the answer if you forget to flip the inequality sign.

**Q7 — A (3 and $-5$).** Zeros are where each factor $=0$: $x-3=0 \Rightarrow x=3$; $x+5=0 \Rightarrow x=-5$.
> **Trap:** (B)/(D) flip the sign of the root instead of setting each factor to zero correctly.

**Q8 — 3.** Vertex $x=-b/(2a)=12/4=3$.
> **Trap:** forgetting the negative sign in the formula, or dividing by $a$ instead of $2a$.

**Q9 — B.** Doubling every 4 hours ⇒ exponent is $t/4$: $P=100\cdot2^{t/4}$.
> **Trap:** (A) puts $t$ in the wrong place (would mean doubling every $\frac14$ hour).

**Q10 — B ($b=8$ or $b=-8$).** One real solution ⇒ discriminant $=0$: $b^2-4(2)(8)=0 \Rightarrow b^2=64 \Rightarrow b=\pm8$.
> **Trap:** forgetting the $\pm$ — both values satisfy the condition.

**Q11 — 0.** $g(-2)=(-2)^3-4(-2)=-8+8=0$.
> **Trap:** sign errors on the cube of a negative number ($(-2)^3=-8$, not $8$).

**Q12 — B (2).** For $y=\frac{a}{x-h}+k$, the horizontal asymptote is $y=k=2$.
> **Trap:** (D) 5 confuses the vertical asymptote ($x=5$) with the horizontal one.

**Q13 — C (\$64.80).** $80(0.75)=60$; $60(1.08)=64.80$.
> **Trap:** (D) applies 8% to the original \$80 instead of the discounted price — each percentage must apply to the correct base.

**Q14 — B (mean 25, SD 4).** Adding a constant shifts the mean but leaves spread unchanged (Shift Rule).
> **Trap:** (C)/(D) assume SD changes too — only *scaling* (multiplying) changes SD, not shifting.

**Q15 — B (65°).** $180-40-75=65$.
> **Trap:** arithmetic slip subtracting in the wrong order.

**Q16 — B ($6\sqrt2$).** 45-45-90 triangle: hypotenuse $=s\sqrt2=6\sqrt2$.
> **Trap:** (C) 12 treats it like doubling instead of applying the $\sqrt2$ ratio.

## Reading & Writing

**Q17 — B.** The passage's point is that the algal partner, not coral genetics, explains survival.
> **Trap:** (A)/(C)/(D) are true-ish details from the passage but not the *main* claim.

**Q18 — B.** The mismatch between projected and actual usage patterns supports this.
> **Trap:** (C)/(D) go beyond what the text supports — no evidence given about future decline or original design intent.

**Q19 — B.** Direct evidence: removing the suspected cause (light) correlates with the effect (recovery) reversing.
> **Trap:** (A) would *undermine* the claim, not support it — reread the direction of each option before matching.

**Q20 — B.** Gains grow (2→5→9→9.5) but the jump from 12→16 weeks (0.5) is much smaller than earlier jumps — rate is slowing.
> **Trap:** (A) ignores the flattening between weeks 12 and 16.

**Q21 — B (cautious).** Context: board members want more disclosure before voting — wariness, not enthusiasm.
> **Trap:** (D) confused doesn't fit — they have a clear, deliberate ask (more disclosures), not confusion.

**Q22 — B (packed with layered meaning).** "Dense with allusion" + rereading to grasp meaning signals content-density, not literal weight.
> **Trap:** (A) is the literal, wrong-context meaning of "dense" — classic words-in-context trap.

**Q23 — B.** Specific incident (one experiment) → broader significance (decade-long research shift).
> **Trap:** (D) mislabels the structure as comparison rather than incident-to-significance.

**Q24 — B.** Economist B's own argument (cyclical, will recover) directly reframes A's "permanent" claim as temporary.
> **Trap:** (A) contradicts Economist B's stated position entirely.

**Q25 — B.** Technical mechanics → general reflection is a broadening move, not a narrowing or correction.
> **Trap:** (D) states the opposite direction of the shift.

**Q26 — B (However,).** Original design assumption vs. later discovery of a flaw = contradiction.
> **Trap:** (D) "As a result," implies a causal consequence, but the corrosion isn't a *result* of the original design — it contradicts the design's assumption.

**Q27 — C (That said,).** Higher resolution (positive) vs. triple processing time (drawback) = a concession/contrast, not a straight cause-effect.
> **Trap:** (A) "Consequently," implies the slower time is a *result* of higher resolution, which isn't stated — it's a separate trade-off, not a proven causal chain.

**Q28 — C.** Only this choice states both the initiative (500 trees) and the measured outcome (6°F drop) — the actual "outcome" the prompt asks for.
> **Trap:** (A) and (D) omit the outcome entirely; (B) is off-topic (describes researchers' general work, not this initiative's result).

**Q29 — A (is).** Subject is "the committee" (singular); "along with its three subcommittees" is a non-essential phrase and doesn't make the subject plural.
> **Trap:** (B) "are" agrees with the nearby "subcommittees," not the true subject — classic buried-subject trap.

**Q30 — A (, and).** Two independent clauses ("The engineers ran... / each trial produced...") joined with comma + FANBOYS.
> **Trap:** (D) "and" alone (no comma) is a common but incorrect IC/IC join.

**Q31 — A (, who / ,).** "who taught high school chemistry for thirty years" is non-essential (deletable without changing the core meaning — we still know whose uncle) and needs commas on both sides.
> **Trap:** (D) treats the clause as essential (restrictive), but there's only one uncle in context — the clause is added information, not identifying information.

**Q32 — B (employees').** Plural subject ("all of the... contracts" belonging to more than one employee) needs the plural possessive: employees'.
> **Trap:** (A) employee's is singular possessive — wrong number; (C) employees's isn't a standard English form.
